# 1l: platform stability (the rare hang, the rewrite-mode stall, veneer room)

Verdict: the rare hang, the crashes of the debugger loop and the rewrite-mode Podman stall had one cause (a Go stack that moved under a buffer handed to the OS as an integer, which made the debugger loop continue the wrong event and freeze the stub), fixed and checked with 300 of 300 fork+exec runs in each of the three modes; rewrite mode now runs the Podman engine as fast as native (`version` after 2 s, not 30-50 s) after a 32 MiB `%fs` veneer area replaced the 448 KiB one that large images filled, a static glibc start-up crash in rewrite mode (an `%fs` instruction split between two pieces of text) is fixed, and stub recycling is not done.

## 1. The rare hang: the debugger loop read the wrong event

Re-running the hang check with the full output kept (the earlier runs cut the head of the crash dump off) showed two failure shapes, both in the debugger loop of a stub:

- A run that ended after 1-2 s with exit code 2 and a Go crash dump: an access violation inside `WaitForDebugEvent`, writing at an address near the goroutine's stack (`Exception 0xc0000005 0x1 ... PC in ntdll`, `debugLoop` line `pWaitDebugEvent.Call(ev.base(), 100)`).
- A run that never ended: the state dump (`RUNSC_HANG_DUMP`) showed a stub with 6 debug events, whose last event was a *second* breakpoint of the anchor thread, handled as "not handled", with `ContinueDebugEvent` failing ("The parameter is incorrect"); the new thread of that stub sat at `RtlUserThreadStart` with 0 cycles, "replaced" once after 3 s and the same again. That is the "redirected thread that does not run" of 1k: the process was frozen by a debug event that was never continued.

Cause: `debugEvent` and `ctxBuf` are handed to the OS as `uintptr` (`ev.base()`), and `ev`/`ctx` were locals of `debugLoop` (and of `interrupt`, `DumpState`). A local of a goroutine lives on its stack, and the stack can be moved (grown) between taking the address and the system call, for example in the prologue of `Proc.Call`. `WaitForDebugEvent` then wrote into the old stack: either an access violation (the crash) or into memory that was still mapped, so that the loop read the previous event from its new stack (the loader breakpoint of the anchor), continued the wrong thread with the wrong status (error 87), and left the real event, for instance the creation of a new thread, unacknowledged. A debug event that is not continued freezes every thread of the process for good. The same window existed for the `uintptr(unsafe.Pointer(&x))` arguments of the `call` helper (a function without `//go:uintptrescapes`).

Fix: `newDebugEvent` and `newCtxBuf` return heap objects (a heap object never moves) and are used by the debugger loop, `interrupt` and `DumpState`; `call` is marked `//go:uintptrescapes`. The shape of the debugger loop did not change: the suspicions of 1k (SetThreadContext against ContinueDebugEvent ordering, an earlier suspension, the anchor thread, capture without SuspendThread) did not matter and the capture without SuspendThread is not needed; the bounded waits of 1k stay as a safety net.

Result: `windows-stability` (new workflow, `workflow_dispatch` input `runs`, fork+exec of busybox `true` 100 times through `runsc.exe`, 16 s limit per run, state dump at 12 s): 300 of 300 in native, 300 of 300 in rewrite and 300 of 300 in rewrite-fxsave (run 45948571238, commit 46c937b), before: 98 of 100 in the exclusive-cache check. The Podman acceptance (`podman-windows`, native and rewrite, 4 runs each) had no `vfork` stop and no hang in any run (before: native 2 of 8 hung in `vforkStop`, rewrite 8 of 8 did not finish); the other failures there are known ones (step 5 `build-apt`: "libc.so.6: file too short" from the host folder; step 9/10 sometimes; the engine's Go crash in `ScanSpanPackedAVX512` at `%fs:-8` in native mode, 2 of 4 runs, which rewrite mode cannot have).

## 2. Podman stalls in rewrite mode: the veneer area was full

With the debugger fixed, rewrite mode finished all runs (4 of 4, native 4 of 4 for the harness, no hang) but was five times slower (`version` after 32-53 s, pull 136-251 s against 21-37 s). The state dump of the engine 20 s after its start (`ci-run.ps1` sets `RUNSC_HANG_DUMP=...:20`) said why: the engine's address space had run 57809 of its 58665 debug events as `%fs` instructions the debugger runs (about 100 us each), the veneer area "used 458744 of 458752 bytes" plus the 226 KB tail pool, 3684 `ud2` sites left, all "veneer area full or veneer not buildable". The hot sites were Go and glibc TLS accesses (`%fs:0x28`-style stack protector loads and stores in libc, `mov %fs:(%rcx),%rax` in the Go runtime). Not the safe zone after piece starts (the reason column shows only "no window of 7 bytes" after the fix), not interrupts inside veneers (stops moved out of veneers: 0-1).

Fixes, in order of effect (task 3 of the brief is the same defect: veneer room):

1. A 32 MiB `%fs` veneer area at 0x4000_0000 (`stubasm.FSBig`), a second section per address space in rewrite mode, committed 64 KiB at a time as it fills (`VirtualAlloc` on the reserved section view, as the control pages), mapped into the stub and reserved against the guest through the memory manager like the other holes (the guest loses 32 MiB at 1 GiB). The engine uses about 1 MB of it. The old area (448 KiB) and the unused end of the syscall veneers' space stay as fallback, and a veneer that ended up out of rel32 reach of its code makes the site a `ud2` instead of a wrong jump. Veneers are 8-byte aligned (were 16).
2. Position-independent executables load at 0x1000_0000 plus up to 256 MiB (`arch.PIELoadBase`, set by the platform at start-up; Linux's 2/3 of the address space is out of reach of the veneers): the main binary of Rust and Go PIE programs is now near, so its `%fs` sites get the 5-byte jump (and windows of 5 bytes, not 7). Libraries still map at the top, far, with the 7-byte form.

Result (engine dump after the fix, `podman-windows` rewrite, 2 runs, steps 1-3): `version` after 2 s, pull 21-35 s, `run` 7-11 s (native: 2 s, 23-37 s, 6-9 s); engine address space 372 debug events of which 87 `%fs` run by the debugger, 233 `ud2` sites, big area 1.0 MB of 32 MB used. All remaining debugger runs are "no window of 7 bytes" sites of high-mapped libraries (the "far none" class of 1j: 1-5% of glibc's sites, executed 87 times in a start-up, not in loops).

Not done: the 24% of far `%fs` sites in Rust PIE binaries are now near (so the table of 1j no longer applies to PIE main binaries); libraries mapped high keep their 1-5% far sites without a 7-byte window (an island near the libraries would remove them). Python3 with libpython (about 8500 sites, 1j) fits the big area (the area holds about 400000 veneers).

## 3. Static glibc crashed at start in rewrite mode

`test/guests/fsstress.c` (static, gcc, `-no-pie`) died in rewrite mode right after `set_tid_address`: SIGSEGV writing at 0x611 at 0x431ffb. Disassembly of that spot (`objdump` in the Ubuntu job): `mov %al,%fs:0x611` is the 8 bytes 0x431ffb-0x432002, and the text is mapped in pieces: 0x42f000-0x432000 and 0x432000-0x433000. The instruction straddles two pieces, so neither piece's scan sees it (the first ends in `64 88 04`, which the decoder cannot read, the second begins with the rest), and it ran natively with a zero `%fs` base. (It is deterministic because of where the linker put it; ADR 0020's check of "every `%fs` instruction is rewritten" was true per piece only.)

Fix: when a piece is mapped in rewrite mode and its last bytes (up to 14) hold the start of an instruction with a `%fs` prefix that continues beyond the end (found by decoding from an instruction boundary with 15 zero bytes appended, `splitFSAt`), nothing in those bytes is patched and the first byte becomes `int3`; the address and the original bytes are remembered (`fsSplit`). The debug event for that `int3` reads the rest of the instruction from the stub (the other piece is mapped by then, or a fetch fault reports it and the thread comes back), decodes the whole instruction and runs it in the thread's own veneer like a `ud2` `%fs` site (`fsSplitRun`, `fsRunInline`), then continues behind it. Tests: `TestSplitFSAt` (decoder level, runs everywhere), `TestFSInstructionSplitAcrossPieces` (a `mov %al,%fs:0x10` cut by a page boundary, both mapping orders); `windows-glibc-diag` runs the real `fsstress`: fails before (exit 139), passes after (`--- PASS fsstress`, two "split" lines in the log). The 1-in-30 "stuck stub" of `windows-runsc` and the rewrite-fxsave `tty` timeout reported by the owner of the run are the hang of section 1 as far as the dumps go (a stub whose next process never starts; the stub waits for a thread that never runs); `windows-runsc` ran without them since, but one run is not 30.

## 4. Address reuse after text unmap

`TestAddressReuseAfterUnmap`: text with a `mov $39,%eax; syscall` site and a relocated site is mapped, run, unmapped and other code (another syscall number, another instruction after the relocated site, plain code where the first site's restart target was) mapped at the same addresses. Checks: no `restartAt` entry in the unmapped range survives; the table entry follows the new syscall number (the per-site entry is keyed by address and checked against the number); the veneer of the relocated site is the one of the new bytes (veneers are keyed by content); entering at the address that was a restart target runs the new code. `purgeSites` also drops `fsSplit`. Passes in all three legs.

## 5. Not done

- Reuse of stub processes across exec (task 5): not started; the cost this run went to the hang and the veneer area. The fork+exec loop is still 1.4 s per 100 in the guests of `windows-runsc` (3.5 s on the dev VM).
- Islands near high-mapped libraries for the 7-byte sites without a window.
- `windows-runsc` showed a nil dereference in `gofer.(*dentry).disownAllChildrenForInvalidation` (negative cache entries in `d.children`) when a host-folder watch ended, which killed the `tty` test in two legs of one run; the same fix arrived from `windows` and was merged.

## Tools added

`windows-stability` (fork+exec N times, three modes, dumps of failures), `windows-glibc-diag` (static glibc guest, disassembly, debug run), `tools/windows/disasm`, the 20 s state dump of the Podman engine (`ci-run.ps1`), the state dump's list of the hottest `%fs` ud2 sites with the reason.

## 6. Follow-ups after the coordinator's second round

- Hangs "not converging" (Podman 3 of 8, dump with `ContinueDebugEvent ok=false`, `replaced threads 3`): that is exactly the signature of section 1 (a loop that continued the wrong event); the run named (45952884948) belongs to a branch without the heap-buffer fix. This branch had no hang in 300+300+300 fork+exec runs and none in 8 Podman engine runs (4 native, 4 rewrite). To make a failed continue loud anyway, the loop now ends the stub (terminates the process, marks it dead, the tasks fail) after three failed `ContinueDebugEvent` in a row. The 3-second "replace a thread that has not run" rule is unchanged: with the cause fixed it fired in none of the runs here; switching it off was not tested.
- Native-mode GC crash (`ScanSpanPackedAVX512`, fault address 0xfffffffffffffff8): seen here in 2 of 4 native Podman runs (the `%fs:-8` load with a zero base). Mitigation, not the `rdfsbase` redesign: the repair path gave up after 20 repairs of one instruction within 5 ms; now 2000 within 20 ms. Not verified (no reproduction without Podman was built; the `rdfsbase`-compare trampoline needs flag-preserving code in the stub and was not started).
- Unpatched syscall across adjacent ranges (the `debian` case, repatching from the seam instead of the 48-byte zone, `ProcessInstrumentationCallback`): not started.
